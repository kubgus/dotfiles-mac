# InVenture Mind - Initiative Story

**Question:** "Tell us about a time you took initiative to create, fix, or improve something."
(max 500 words)

**Situation:** Behavioural question, first of the application. Kubo wrote his own draft first,
so this file is also the best English-language sample of his unedited voice.

---

## His own draft - unedited

> I volunteer as a scout troop leader at Scouting Slovakia. I joined the troop council during a
> large decline in members and there was a real risk it would collapse from a lack of registered
> members. I decided to take initiative because I felt an obligation to a troop that helped raise
> me. During the last three months of the year, I facilitated the creation of a new group with the
> help of the rest of the council by running a recruitment campaign. This involved communication
> with children's parents, handling official registration forms and fees, and synchronizing with
> multiple other troops to be able to meet weekly with the new group. Since then, we have expanded
> our council team and the troop itself, creating three additional groups and recruiting and
> mentoring new volunteers to lead them.

### The voice in it - preserve these

- Flat, unadorned declaratives. No rhetorical build, no callbacks.
- States a motive in one clause and moves on, without dressing it up.
- Modest to the point of self-erasure. Real, and worth keeping *somewhere* - just not in the
  sentence that answers the question.

### What needed fixing

1. **~160 of 500 words.** The missing 340 were exactly the selection criteria: how he decided,
   what was hard, what he tried that had not been tried.
2. **"I facilitated the creation of a new group with the help of the rest of the council."**
   "Facilitated" is the verb people use when they do not want to claim the thing - fatal in an
   *initiative* question. Keep the modesty, move it off the load-bearing sentence.
3. **"I felt an obligation"** reads as duty-bound, almost reluctant, against a program selecting
   for curiosity and motivation. Same fact, wrong energy: the troop was worth saving and nobody
   else was going to.
4. **No numbers anywhere.** "A large decline", "a new group", "three additional groups."
5. **Nothing about the insight.** The recruitment campaign was one clause. What he did that the
   council had not already tried is the part that answers "potential to develop new ideas."
6. **Ends in a subordinate clause.** "Recruiting and mentoring new volunteers to lead them" means
   the thing runs without him - the strongest fact in the answer, buried.

## Final - sent

> I volunteer as a scout troop leader at Scouting Slovakia. I joined the troop council in 2022, at
> a point when membership had fallen to fewer than 10 registered members and the troop was close
> to being deregistered entirely. Nobody on the council had the time to take on recruitment, and
> the troop is the reason I grew up the way I did, so I took it. I had three months before the
> registration deadline. I noticed that the troop wasn't failing because kids didn't want to join;
> it was failing because there was no entry point for new ones. Creating one meant starting a new
> group from nothing rather than trying to refill the existing ones. Over those months I ran a
> recruitment campaign that brought in around 10 children, enough to start a new group. I handled
> communication with the children's parents, the official registration forms and fees, and the
> scheduling with several other troops so the new group could meet weekly. Since then we have
> expanded both the council and the troop itself, founding three additional groups and recruiting
> and mentoring the new volunteers who lead them. The part I'm most glad about is that the troop
> is no longer dependent on any one person doing what I did. It now has 30+ members.

### What changed

- Opens on the stake (fewer than 10 members, close to deregistration), context demoted.
- "I felt an obligation" became "the troop is the reason I grew up the way I did, so I took it" -
  his register exactly, and a reason rather than a duty.
- Added the diagnosis sentence (no entry point), which is what turns effort into judgement.
- "Facilitated" replaced with the verbs for what he actually did: ran, handled, negotiated.
- Numbers throughout, bracketed as `[N]` in the draft handed back so he filled them himself.
- Self-sustaining outcome moved to the last two sentences.

### Grammar fixes worth remembering

Recurring slips in his English, all present in the draft he had nearly sent:

- **"the troop wasn't falling"** → *failing*. Changes the meaning, easy to miss.
- **Broken parallelism in lists:** "I handled the communication..., handling official registration
  forms..., and synchronizing with..." Three different forms in one series. His most visible
  recurring error - check every list.
- **"less than 10 members"** → *fewer than* (countable).
- Comma splices between two independent clauses; doubled relative pronouns ("...campaign, which
  brought in 10 children, which was enough...").
