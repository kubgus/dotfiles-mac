# InVenture Mind - Why Me

**Question:** "Please let us know why you should be part of InVenture Mind." (max 500 words)

**Situation:** The question that decides the application, for a program whose stated criteria are
"curious and motivated students who want to work on themselves." Kubo's CV is unusual for the
cohort - it recruits high school seniors and says no business background is needed - so the risk
is reading as *why does he need us*.

**This file is the reference for what "in my voice" means.** Draft 1 was rejected by him with:
*"write less about what you have in your system prompt and more honestly about my motivation."*

---

## Draft 1 - rejected

> I have spent the last two years building software that other people specified. At Wezeo I
> worked as a backend developer on four production projects in teams of up to ten. Since May I
> have been leading a team of four at Vedecké okienko, building a learning platform from scratch,
> where I own the scope, the technical specifications and the standards the team works to.
>
> So I know how to build something, and how to get a small team to build it with me. What I have
> never done is find out whether anyone wants it. [...]
>
> I also think I would be useful to the people I am placed with. In a group of a hundred students
> there will be people with good ideas who cannot yet build them [...] I have mentored kids aged
> 7 to 17 at CoderDojo, and in my scout troop I recruited and trained the volunteers who now lead
> its groups. I am comfortable being the person who makes a team work rather than the person with
> the loudest idea in it.
>
> I am not applying for the certificate. I am applying because I have become good at the part of
> the work that comes second, and I would like to be taught the part that comes first.

### Why it failed

- **It opens with a CV paste.** Employer, role, headcount, scope of ownership - four profile
  facts before a single sentence of motivation. Every one was true and none was load-bearing.
- **It argues a case rather than saying a thing.** The structure is credentials → gap → ask →
  what-I-offer → closer. That is a cover letter, and it reads as one.
- **The closer is a rhetorical construction.** "The part that comes second / the part that comes
  first" is a good line that he would not have written. Performance, not speech.
- It was also the *second* draft in a row with this problem - the same habit produced a
  well-organised, slightly hollow answer both times.

---

## Draft 2 - sent

Written from one line he gave: *"i want to explore and learn how to build from a-z instead of
making tools nobody wants."* Single block, no paragraph breaks (Google Form).

> I want to learn how to build something from start to finish. So far everything I have built has
> been someone else's idea - a client's, a company's, a school's - and my job was to make it work,
> not to decide whether it should exist. I have become good at shipping things without ever
> knowing whether they were worth shipping, and that bothers me more the longer I do it. I do not
> want to spend the next ten years making tools nobody wants. What I am missing is everything that
> happens before the code: finding a problem that is actually a problem, talking to the people who
> have it, being wrong about what they need and finding that out early rather than four months in.
> I cannot teach myself that from documentation, which is how I have learned almost everything
> else I know. It takes people who have already done it and other people to try it alongside,
> which is what this program is. I think I would also be useful to work with. There will be people
> there with ideas they cannot build yet, and building is the part I can already do. I am applying
> because I want to be bad at something again, with someone around to ask.

### Why it works

- **Zero profile facts.** No employer, no role, no headcount. The only claim carried over is the
  implicit one that he can build - and it is load-bearing, because the offer to teammates depends
  on it. Everything else was dropped.
- **Opens on the want, not the résumé.** The first sentence is the actual motivation, in his own
  words, barely edited.
- **The weakness is the argument.** "I have become good at shipping things without ever knowing
  whether they were worth shipping" is simultaneously the credential and the gap, in one clause,
  with no boast.
- **"That bothers me more the longer I do it"** is the sentence a person writes and a draft does
  not. Keep the ones like it.
- **The closer is plain.** "I want to be bad at something again, with someone around to ask" -
  same job as the rejected closer, no rhetorical scaffolding.
- ~215 words of 500. Short because it had nothing left to say, not because it ran out.

## Reusable test

Before sending anything written as him, count the profile facts. If the answer would collapse
without them, it is a CV. If they could all be deleted and the answer still says the same thing,
delete them.
