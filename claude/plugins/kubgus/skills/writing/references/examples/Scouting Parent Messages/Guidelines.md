# Scouting Parent Messages - Guidelines

Parent-facing communication for the troop ([ZBOR], [ODDIEL]). Two sub-genres live here:

- **Enrollment / inquiry replies** - see `System Prompt.md`, a full assistant prompt + few-shot set for answering parents interested in joining.
- **Announcements & crisis comms** - cancellations, changes, logistics to already-enrolled families (often WhatsApp). See the worked examples below.

## Register

- **Slovak, vykanie to parents** ("vám", "veríme", "ďakujeme"). Warm and respectful, never stiff or bureaucratic.
- **Signed by the collective** ("-Oddielová rada") for official troop decisions, not by an individual leader - it's the troop council speaking, and it shields any one leader from being the face of an unpopular call.
- **Capitalize `Vy` by addressee, not by formality.** A WhatsApp message to the whole group is
  many people: lowercase `vám`, `vás`, `vaša`. An email to one parent is a single person:
  capital `Vám`, `Vás`, `Váš`.
- **Sign-off follows the channel.** WhatsApp announcements end `-Oddielová rada`. Emails end
  `Prajem pekný deň,` and let the mail client append the signature - no council line there.
- **The troop's people are `vedúci`, `radcovia`, `oddielová rada`** - never `kolegovia`, `tím`
  or `personál`. Workplace vocabulary turns a volunteer troop into an institution, which is
  also the coldest way to hand a parent off to someone else: name the people, not the unit.
- Plain hyphen `-` only. A single light emoji (`:)`) at the end is on-brand and keeps a hard message human.

## Announcement / cancellation structure

When calling off or changing an event, in this order:

1. **Lead with the decision.** State plainly what's cancelled/changed up front - parents need the actionable fact before the reasoning.
2. **Give the honest reason** without over-explaining. Both real causes, named ("náhly pokles v záujme v prihláške a poveternostná situácia").
3. **Own the part that was on us.** If the troop created uncertainty, name it and validate the parents' reaction ("uvedomujeme si, že sme tento fakt nedali jasne najavo a vyvolali vo vás oprávnený pocit neistoty"). Accountability without grovelling.
4. **Reframe forward.** Position the call as the wiser one and gesture at a better next time ("snáď nabudúce dostaneme aj krajšie počasie").
5. **Thank for understanding**, sign off as the council.

## Interim messages - writing before the answer exists

The hardest ones are sent *while* a decision is still being made: parents are already waiting,
and the thing they want to know is not decided yet. Rules that hold across both channels:

1. **Payload above the fold.** WhatsApp collapses long messages behind "Read more". One
   apology sentence, then dated bullets of what happens when. Reasoning goes underneath.
2. **Put a date on every promise.** "V nedeľu" makes the reader rebuild the calendar; `13.09.`
   does not.
3. **Say what is *not* happening too.** A cancelled week that parents have to infer from a
   start date re-creates the ambiguity you are writing to fix.
4. **Promise a floor you can beat, not a ceiling you can miss.** "Najneskôr začiatkom budúceho
   týždňa" beats "už túto nedeľu". A second missed deadline costs far more than the first.
5. **Never open a feedback window you cannot adjudicate.** Inviting parents to object to a
   decision without stating the criteria produces conflicting requests and makes every refusal
   look arbitrary. Acknowledge the worry, defer the process.
6. **Give the delay a real cause.** A named constraint ("prebieha reorganizácia družín") is a
   reason; an unexplained apology reads as evasion.
7. **Own harm without hedging it.** "Vyvolali sme vo vás neistotu", not "mohli sme spôsobiť".
   But apologise once - a second apology paragraph weakens the first.
