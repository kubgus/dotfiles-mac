# Writing as Kubo

## The core rule - write as him, not about him

The failure this exists to prevent is drafting from his profile like a CV. `references/profile.md`
describes him so you recognise what he means, not so it can be recited back as credentials.

- **Use the one fact the sentence needs, drop the rest.** A list of roles reads as a CV
  paste-in. One role, named because the argument turns on it, reads as a person talking.
  If the piece survives deleting every profile fact, delete them.
- **Understated beats impressive.** "I have become good at shipping things without ever
  knowing whether they were worth shipping" lands. "I lead a team of four and own the
  technical specs" is the same fact performed.
- **An admitted weakness beats a polished strength.** Panels and readers have seen a
  thousand strengths. The honest gap is the differentiator.
- **One flat true sentence beats a constructed arc.** No rhetorical build, no callback
  ending, no three-part rhythm. If a line reads as performance rather than as something he
  would have typed at a keyboard, cut it.
- **A fact may be used or softened, never extrapolated.** Hand a gap back as a bracket
  (`[N]`, `[which year?]`) rather than filling it plausibly.
- **Every added sentence must carry news.** A pile-up of intensifiers is the tell that one
  does not.

## Editing his prose

Preserve voice: match register, rhythm and idiom, and never rewrite a draft into generic
AI prose. Show the change and say why. The thoroughness rules that govern answers written
*to* him do not govern prose written *as* him.

Read `references/examples/Scouting Parent Messages/Voice Reference - Unedited Originals.md`
before editing his Slovak - it holds messages written entirely by him with no AI
involvement, and separates which habits are the voice from which are drafting slips. Every
other example is already an edited result, so matching those compounds whatever drift they
introduced.

## Structure that tends to be wrong on the first pass

- **Lead with the stake, not the context.** The situation goes in the subordinate clause.
- **Numbers or nothing.** "A large decline" is free to write and worth nothing. Bracket
  the figures only he has rather than dropping the slot.
- **End on the fact that carries weight.** Drafts trail off into subordinate clauses, and
  the strongest fact is usually buried there. Move it to the last sentence.
- **Diagnose before prescribing.** Name why the thing was broken, then what you did about
  it. That is what makes a paragraph read as judgement rather than effort.

## Language and register

Slovak native, English and Czech bilingual. Match the register the relationship already
established rather than the one the genre suggests - do not formalize a thread that is
already casual, or over-familiarize one that is not. **Ask when register or language is
ambiguous**, through AskUserQuestion.

Slovak specifics: capitalize `Vy` by addressee, not by formality - a group message takes
lowercase `vám`, a message to one person takes capital `Vám`. Watch subject-verb agreement
on collective nouns (`tím` is singular).

## Genre guidelines

Each folder under `references/examples/` opens with a `Guidelines.md` that owns its genre.
Read the matching one before drafting:

| Genre | Folder |
|---|---|
| Application and motivation answers, scholarships, accelerators | `Application & Motivation Answers/` |
| Recruiter threads, declining offers, hiring messages | `Recruiter & Job Messages/` |
| Parent-facing scouting announcements, cancellations, enrollment | `Scouting Parent Messages/` |
| Blog posts, technical-but-personal | `Personal Blog/` |
| School assignments | `School Assignments/` |

Examples carry `[PLACEHOLDER]` tokens where a real name, address or organisation was
removed. Keep them as placeholders; do not invent values to fill them.
