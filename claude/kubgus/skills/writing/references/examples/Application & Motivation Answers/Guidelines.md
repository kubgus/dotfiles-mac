# Application & Motivation Answers - Guidelines

English free-text answers on application forms: programs, scholarships, accelerators, job
applications. First person, written **as** Kubo. Usually a word cap and a Google Form text box.

## The core rule - write as him, not about him

`SKILL.md` owns the rule; this genre is where it bites hardest. He caught it directly: the
good draft happened because it "didn't use the typical AI banter and didn't blindly parrot
things I've told you about myself." Most of these programs say outright that they want
people who "want to work on themselves", so the honest gap is the differentiator here.

## Register

- Plain declaratives, mostly short. States a reason flatly and moves on: *"the troop is the
  reason I grew up the way I did, so I took it."*
- **Name the counter-argument to your own idea.** The strongest 30 words in the study-interest
  answer are the objection to it. Anyone can say a field is exciting; naming what makes it hard
  separates interest from enthusiasm.

## Structure

`SKILL.md` owns the structure rules. A figure counts most in this genre: "fallen to fewer
than 10 registered members" is credibility at the cost of one clause.

## Question types - they are not all worth the same effort

- **Persuasion questions** (the initiative story, "why you") decide the application. Spend here.
- **Screening/data questions** (competitions, test scores, eligibility) collect a data point.
  A vague claim is *worse than the honest opt-out*: "I participated in programming competitions
  as part of IT studies" has no name, year, scale or result, so an evaluator downgrades it to
  nothing and adds a tint of reaching-for-credit. Name it with a year and a field size, or take
  the opt-out clean with no softener appended.
- Before drafting any of them, **fetch the program's own page** and write against its stated
  selection criteria. Also check eligibility and the deadline before spending an hour on prose.

## Form mechanics

- **Google Forms:** ask whether the field keeps line breaks. Default to one unbroken block for
  anything under ~250 words, and make sure the prose holds up either way.
- **Word caps are ceilings, not targets** - but an answer at a third of the cap is usually
  leaving the selection criteria unaddressed rather than being admirably concise.

## Worked examples

All from the InVenture Mind application (Georgia Tech / Digitálna koalícia, Sep 2026).

- `InVenture Mind - Why Me.md` - **read this one first.** Holds both drafts: the overengineered
  CV-recital and the honest rewrite that replaced it. The contrast is the whole lesson.
- `InVenture Mind - Initiative Story.md` - his own raw draft, the critique, and the final. The
  best reference for his unedited voice in English.
- `InVenture Mind - What Would You Study.md` - writing an answer from a one-line prompt, and
  where the line between using his profile and inventing from it sits.
