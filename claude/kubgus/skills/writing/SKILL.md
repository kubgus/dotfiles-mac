# Writing as Kubo

## Write as him, not about him

The failure this exists to prevent is drafting from his profile like a CV.
`references/profile.md` is there so you recognise what he means, not so it can be recited
back as credentials.

- **Use the one fact the sentence needs, drop the rest.** A list of roles reads as a CV
  paste-in. One role, named because the argument turns on it, reads as a person talking.
  If the piece survives deleting every profile fact, delete them.
- **Understated beats impressive.** "I have become good at shipping things without ever
  knowing whether they were worth shipping" lands. "I lead a team of four and own the
  technical specs" is the same fact performed.
- **An admitted weakness beats a polished strength.** Readers have seen a thousand
  strengths. The honest gap is the differentiator.
- **One flat true sentence beats a constructed arc.** No rhetorical build, no callback
  ending, no three-part rhythm. If a line reads as performance rather than as something he
  would have typed, cut it.
- **Use or soften a fact, never extrapolate it.** Hand a gap back as a bracket - `[N]`,
  `[which year?]` - rather than filling it plausibly.
- **Every added sentence carries news.** A pile-up of intensifiers is the tell that one
  does not.

## Editing his prose

Match register, rhythm and idiom. Never rewrite a draft into generic AI prose. Show the
change and say why. The thoroughness rules that govern answers written *to* him do not
govern prose written *as* him.

Before editing his Slovak, read
`references/examples/Scouting Parent Messages/Voice Reference - Unedited Originals.md` -
written entirely by him, and it separates which habits are the voice from which are
drafting slips. Every other example is an edited result, so matching those compounds
whatever drift they already carry.

## Structure, usually wrong on the first pass

- **Lead with the stake, not the context.** The situation goes in the subordinate clause.
- **End on the fact that carries weight.** Drafts trail off into subordinate clauses, and
  the strongest fact is usually buried in one. Move it to the last sentence.
- **Diagnose before prescribing.** Name why the thing was broken, then what was done about
  it. That is what makes a paragraph read as judgement rather than effort.
- **Prefer a figure, but never force one.** "A large decline" is free to write and worth
  nothing. A real number is best, a bracket he can fill (`[N]`) is next, and an honest
  vague quantifier - "most of the troop", "fewer than half" - beats a number that was
  never counted. Inventing precision is worse than admitting its absence.

## Language and register

Slovak native, English and Czech bilingual. Match the register the relationship already
established, not the one the genre suggests: do not formalise a thread that is already
casual, or over-familiarise one that is not. **Ask through AskUserQuestion when register
or language is ambiguous.**

Slovak specifics:

- Capitalise `Vy` by addressee, not by formality. A group message takes lowercase `vám`; a
  message to one person takes capital `Vám`.
- Collective nouns are singular - `tím` takes `rozhodol`, not `rozhodli`.
- Dates take a space after each period: `13. 09. 2026`, not `13.09.2026`.
- Dates are `DD. MM. YYYY`, with a space after each period. `13. 09. 2026`, not `13.09.2026`.

## Genre guidelines

Each folder under `references/examples/` opens with a `Guidelines.md` that owns its genre.
Read the matching one before drafting.

| School assignments | `School Assignments/` |

removed. Keep them as placeholders; never invent values to fill them.
